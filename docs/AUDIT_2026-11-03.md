workspace data, and the path from guided collection to an AirPods-only model.

## 1. What already existed and works

- **Collect (iOS)**: guided recordings with timed instructions, the
  "Collect Required Data" list, on-device quality checks, AirPods dropout
  pause/resume, coarse chewing-span review on the phone, and TLS upload to the
  Mac. Video presentation times are converted to the host clock
  (`CMSyncConvertTime`) and motion uses host-uptime timestamps. One physical
  timing check measured video-minus-motion residuals of −21.8, +18.3 and
  +85.3 ms. This has been checked on one device and one timing take only.
- **Mac loop service**: verified import, immutable sources, working copies,
  Vision, synchronization assessment, review tasks, experiment catalog and
  planner. It is running (`com.chewsense.loop`) and has processed all 3
  physical recordings.
- **Vision (MediaPipe face + hand landmarker)**: per-frame jaw/lip/blendshape
  signals, closed–open–closed chew-cycle proposals and hand-to-mouth
  food-entry proposals.
- **Review (macOS)**: candidate-bound review, blind benchmark mode, immutable
  revisions and finalization.
- **Python checks**: 167 tests passed before this audit and 182 pass after it.

## 2. Problems found

### Training and evaluation

1. **No motion model could be trained on any existing data.**
   `train_temporal_model` raises *"temporal training split has no reviewed
   observable chew events"*. Locked temporal splits also require ≥3 eating
   and ≥3 hard-negative sessions with fully reviewed individual chews. There
   are zero such sessions. As a result, detecting eating (Objective A) was
   blocked behind reviewing every chew (Objective B), even though about
   75 minutes of human coarse-labelled AirPods motion exist.
2. **The materializer aborted the whole dataset when one recording was bad.**
   It stopped on the symlinked `Eating-fieldtest-20260525-185356`, a migrated
   copy of a field test. Fixed with `--skip-invalid`, and the strict error
   now explains how to continue.
3. **Historical model metrics should not be trusted.** Most May 2026 registry
   entries were scored on split `all`, which includes the training data, or
   on one or two sessions. The model bundled in Live
   (`chewnet-20260503-134131-816cea53`) was trained on 6 field tests
   (251 s, one eating session) and validated on a single recording. Its
   eating labels were model output that the wearer accepted unless corrected.
4. **The deployable TCN does not generalize across recording setups** (§6). In
   development it matches gradient boosting, but it misses most chewing in
   the 2026 field tests.

### Labeling

5. **Coarse eating labels are 100% manual.** Vision proposes chews only
   inside spans the wearer has already marked as chewing; hypotheses outside
   those spans are discarded. No video component proposes the spans
   themselves.
6. **Chew-candidate confidence carries no information.** It is one of four
   constants set by heuristic type (0.8, 0.75, 0.6, 0.4). All 32 proposals in
   the guided baseline score 0.75, so "low-confidence" review prioritization
   cannot work.
7. **Talking rejection depends on audio, which Collect turns off by default.**
   Speech status is therefore `unknown` for guided recordings.
8. **Labels are verified one chew at a time.** A session counts as
   "verified" only after a decision on every candidate plus a full-video
   pass. Evaluation sessions also need blind annotation of every chew. This
   is appropriate for a chew-count benchmark, but it is the bottleneck for
   everything else.

### Data

9. Available data: 32 usable recordings, all from one wearer. Six more were
   rejected with reasons (no labels, a duplicate, a corrupt capture, a
   diagnostic take, or conflicting scenario metadata).
10. **Most chewing labels have unverified clocks.** 1,095 of the 1,222
    chewing windows come from 2025 in-app labels whose clock origin is the
    first motion row. Only 48 chewing windows have a verified shared clock.
11. Missing entirely: drinking, gum, talking while eating, varied chewing
    pace, non-eating mouth movements, natural meals, and any second
    person. Talking has 10 labelled windows.
12. The 2025 "Not-eating" recordings carry no activity description, so their
    false positives cannot be attributed to a cause.

## 3. Changes made

**Chewing-bout track** (`pipelines/motion/pipeline/eating/`,
`chewsense-motion eating-experiment`):

- **Label tiers.** Every labelled second keeps its human source tier:
  `human_coarse`, `wearer_no_food_attestation`, `legacy_human_coarse`,
  `human_marked_segment` or `session_declared_negative`. Guided
  instructions, vision proposals, `automatic` sources, unmarked field-test
  time and `unknown` spans never become labels.
- **Windows.** Causal 3 s windows are built with Live's resampler. A ±1 s
  guard band around each label transition is excluded, and so are windows
  with less than 90% sensor coverage.
- **Features.** Interpretable, IMU-only: band powers, dominant frequency,
  spectral entropy and chew-lag autocorrelation for each axis and for the
  gyro and accelerometer magnitudes.
- **Candidate models.** A prior, motion-energy-only, logistic regression,
  gradient boosting, and `TemporalChewNet` trained on its eating head only.
- **Splits.** Session-level, persistent and immutable
  (`workspace/experiments/eating/splits.json`). The newest quarter of each
  stratum becomes the test set, and loop evaluation-role recordings always
  go to test. Development uses grouped 5-fold cross-validation. The test set
  is scored once per full run with the threshold chosen in development.
  `--dev-only` runs never touch it, and the run index counts how many times
  the test set has been consulted.
- **Evaluation.** Precision, recall, F1, false-positive and false-negative
  rates, AUC-ROC/PR, 95% session-bootstrap intervals, threshold sweeps and
  confusion matrices; breakdowns by activity, label tier and recording; bout
  replay through Live's EMA + hysteresis `ChewDetector`, including onset
  latency and false bouts per hour; inference latency.
- **Report.** A self-contained HTML report with a session selector. Each
  timeline shows guided instructions (context only), human spans, the
  probability from the selected model and the TCN, decoded bouts,
  false-positive and false-negative lanes, and the AirPods rotation rate. It
  also lists labels worth a second look, recording suggestions, TCN loss
  curves, model coefficients, run history, and the methods and limits.

**Integration.** The loop report now carries an `eating_detector` summary.
Collect's pipeline progress gains an "AirPods chewing detector
(experimental)" stage with no local paths, and Review → Pipeline gains a panel
with an *Open experiment report* button. The detector is descriptive only and
gates nothing.

**Collection catalog.** Four new guided experiments: `talking-while-eating`,
`gum-chewing`, `chewing-pace` and `facial-expressions`. Instructions remain
intended behaviour only.

**Labeling research tool.** `chewsense-motion video-teacher-eval`.

**Robustness.** The materializer gained a skip-and-report mode, and two run
IDs created in the same second no longer collide.

## 5. Labeling assessment

**Automated today:** chew-cycle and food-entry *proposals* inside
hand-marked chewing spans, per-frame mouth and head signals, a
synchronization-marker check, and review-task highlighting.

**Manual today:** every coarse chewing span (marked on the phone), every
chew decision, a full-video pass per session, blind annotation of every chew
in evaluation sessions, and clock confirmation.

**Measured pilot.** Ungated Vision output on 8 recordings with human labels
(455 face-visible windows, 127 of them chewing;
`workspace/experiments/video-teacher-20261103/video_teacher.json`):

| Signal vs human spans | AUC-ROC | At default rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Video chew-cycle rate (≥2 cycles in 3 s) | 0.88 | precision 0.83, recall 0.65, false-positive rate 5.2% |
| Video jaw-opening periodicity | 0.92 | — |

- **Video errors:** crunchy-food chewing recall was 0.32, and head shaking
  produced a 31% false-positive rate.
- **Motion errors:** nodding produced a 17% false-positive rate, which
  video did not share.
- **Agreement:** the two sources agreed on **82%** of windows. In those
  windows they matched the human label in **374 of 374** (100%: 82
  chewing, 292 not chewing).

The sample is small (8 recordings, one wearer) and the result is not
validated. Still, it is the clearest path to less labeling: propose coarse
spans automatically where independent video and motion evidence agree, and
ask the wearer to review only the disagreements (about 18% of windows here).
Agreed proposals must stay *proposed* until accepted, must never become
evaluation truth, and must not be the only training labels for the motion
model, or the motion model would partly label itself.

**Cadence (exploratory).** Inside human chewing spans, the dominant gyro
frequency and the video jaw-opening frequency differed by a median of
0.06 Hz; 70% of 46 six-second windows were within 0.2 Hz, and both medians
were about 1.5 Hz. All chewing was at one self-selected pace, so
correlation is uninformative (r = 0.15). Whether motion tracks *changes* in
cadence is untested; the new `chewing-pace` experiment targets that.

## 6. Training results (`eating-20261104-014315`)

- **Target:** whether a 3 s causal window lies inside a span the wearer
  marked as chewing. This is not a bite and not a chew count.
- **Data:** 32 recordings, 1 participant, 200,220 motion samples, 24
  human chewing spans, and 7,148 labelled windows, of which 1,222 are
  chewing.
- **Development set:** 23 recordings; 7 contain chewing.
- **Locked test set:** 9 recordings; 3 contain chewing, and 8 have scorable
  windows (127 chewing and 707 non-chewing windows). It contains all 2026
  field tests, the guided baseline and the newest 2025 negative, so it also
  tests generalization forward in time across app and device changes.

| Model | Dev F1 [95% CI] | Dev FPR | Test F1 [95% CI] | Test recall | Test FPR | Test bouts found | Test false bouts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prior | 0.30 | 100% | 0.26 | 1.00 | 100% | — | — |
| Motion energy only | 0.36 | 68% | 0.17 | 0.50 | 80% | — | — |
| Logistic regression | 0.85 [0.72–0.90] | 3.3% | 0.64 [0.23–0.77] | 0.71 | 9.1% | 7/7 | 2 |
| **Gradient boosting** (selected on dev AUC-PR) | **0.87 [0.74–0.92]** | **3.0%** | **0.90 [0.51–0.98]** | **1.00** | **4.1%** | **7/7** | **2** |
| Causal TCN (eating head) | 0.87 [0.78–0.91] | 2.5% | 0.50 [0.00–0.79] | 0.40 | 3.7% | 2/7 | 2 |

- **Development AUC-PR:** gradient boosting 0.944, TCN 0.928, motion energy
  0.158.
- **Selected model (gradient boosting):**
  - development: median bout-onset latency 3.6 s; 8 false bouts, or about
    10 per hour of labelled non-chewing time;
  - test: onset latency 2.1 s; 2 false bouts in 7 minutes of non-chewing;
  - inference: 4.2 ms per window on this Mac, excluding feature
    extraction.

How to read this:

- **Chewing is genuinely detectable from AirPods motion for this wearer.**
  Motion energy alone performs at or below chance, so the models are not
  just detecting "moving more".
- **The test interval is wide** (F1 0.51–0.98) because only 3 test
  recordings contain chewing. Treat it as a pilot estimate, not a
  validated accuracy.
- **The TCN fails in both directions on the 2026 recordings.** It misses
  chewing in the field tests (0 of 53 chewing windows in
  `FieldTest-20260525-185356`) and over-fires on the guided baseline's rest
  and nod windows (false-positive rates of 18% and 59%). Those recordings
  have about half the rotation energy of the 2025 training data, so the
  raw-axis TCN is probably tied to the 2025 recording setup. This is a
  hypothesis, not a confirmed cause. The feature model is more robust
  because most of its features are relative (band fractions,
  periodicity). **Do not choose a fix by its test score.** Instead, add
  2026 training-role recordings and re-evaluate.
- **Sensitivity checks (development only, test untouched):** a ±2 s guard
  gives F1 0.869 and 5 s windows give 0.870. Sub-second legacy clock error
  is therefore not driving the result.

## 7. Error analysis (selected model)

- **In-meal pauses are the main development error.** The false-positive rate
  is 7.5% on not-chewing time inside 2025 meals, versus 2.6% in 2025
  non-eating recordings.
- **`Not-eating-20251212-175356`:** a 19.6% false-positive rate and 3 false
  bouts. The independently trained TCN fires in the same places (about
  15–25 s, 30–40 s and 60–70 s). Either the recording contains chewing or
  speech, or both models share a confound. No video exists, so the
  report's review queue lists these spans rather than assuming either way.
- **`Eating-20251123-055649`:** chewing recall 0.77, with confident misses
  (p ≈ 0.01–0.02) at 15.5–22.5 s and 157.5–164.5 s inside spans labelled
  chewing. These are likely pauses inside broadly marked spans.
- **Test talking:** 2 of 10 windows were flagged as chewing. This is far
  too few windows to estimate a rate.
- **Test nodding (`FieldTest-20260503-063836`):** a 16.7% false-positive
  rate and 1 false bout. Head shaking and walking had 0%.

Each of these is linked to its timeline in
`workspace/experiments/eating/runs/eating-20261104-014315/report.html`.

## 8. What to record next

The collection catalog orders these automatically; the report recomputes the
list after each run.

1. **`talking` and `talking-while-eating`.** They test the worst-measured
   confound (10 labelled windows) and the most realistic one.
2. **`baseline-90s` and `crunchy-food` / `soft-food`.** Most important is
   ≥300 chewing windows with a verified shared clock (48 today), recorded on
   the *current* setup so the TCN's cross-setup failure can be fixed and
   measured.
3. **`head-movement`.** Nodding is the motion detector's largest test error.
4. **`drinking`, `gum-chewing`, `facial-expressions`.** These confounds have
   zero recordings today.
5. **`chewing-pace`.** Needed before any cadence claim.
6. **A natural meal** (manual Eating capture, then mark chewing spans): real
   bout lengths, pauses and conversation.

## 9. Remaining gaps before a reliable AirPods-only system

- **One wearer.** Cross-person, cross-device and AirPods-fit generalization
  are unmeasured.
- **Small test set.** 3 test recordings contain chewing; no natural meals
  and no free-living data exist.
- **Most positives have unverified clocks** (2025 labels).
- **No detector runs on device.** The gradient-boosting model needs its
  features ported to Swift (or a CoreML pipeline). The TCN, which would run
  in Live, does not generalize yet. Live still bundles the unvalidated
  May 2026 MLP.
- **Bite, chew-count and cadence claims are unsupported.** No individual
  chew events have been independently annotated (0 of the 300 the existing
  gates require).
- **Speech can't be identified.** Collect's default is no audio, so Vision
  has no speech signal.
- **The running loop service and Review app still use pre-audit code.** They
  pick up the new stage and panel only after a restart.

## 10. Next priorities

1. Record the requested experiments above. After each coarse review, rerun
   `chewsense-motion eating-experiment` to re-check development, then
   confirm on test.
2. **Agreement-based coarse proposals.** Offer motion+video agreement spans
   as *proposed* coarse labels in Collect's review, so the wearer only
   resolves disagreements. Measure the wearer's review time against marking
   everything by hand.
3. **On-device detection.** Port the 80 window features to Swift and export
   the gradient-boosting model to CoreML. Check Python/Swift parity on
   recorded windows, then run a physical Live smoke test before any
   promotion.
4. **Fix the TCN.** Retrain with current-setup data, and try
   amplitude-robust normalization judged on development data only.
5. **Prioritize review with real uncertainty.** Replace constant chew
   confidences with evidence-based scores, and feed the report's
   disagreement queue into review tasks.
6. **Recruit a second wearer** before any generalization claim.
