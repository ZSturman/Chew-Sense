# Validity study of AirPods chewing detection — November 10, 2026

Scope: can the existing recordings support the claim that AirPods motion
detects chewing, and does the pipeline measure that honestly? Everything
below was run on real recordings of **one wearer**. Synthetic data was used
only in software tests.

Rules kept:
- **Locked test untouched.** No test recording was windowed, fitted or
  scored. Consultations are still 3. `splits.json` gained one entry (the
  Nov 9 baseline, development by its loop role); no existing entry changed.
- **Nothing deployed.** Live still bundles `eating-hgb-20261104-015911-7a28dd15`.
  No model was exported, promoted or installed, and no service was restarted.
- **No recording, label or earlier run was modified.**

Reproduce with the commands at the end. Raw results:
`workspace/experiments/eating/validity/validity-20261110-152247/` (study),
`workspace/experiments/eating/runs/eating-20261110-151121/` (reference run),
`workspace/experiments/eating/prospective/` (forward test).

## 1. What the evidence supports

| Claim | Status | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chewing changes AirPods motion measurably | **Demonstrated, one wearer** | 12–25 Hz amplitude is 4–5× higher while chewing than in pauses of the same meal (§3) |
| A model separates chewing from not chewing in a meal it never saw | **Demonstrated, 5 sittings** | Within-meal AUC-ROC 0.965; time-shift null never above 0.64 in 500 draws (§2) |
| The detector works on a later recording, other app version | **One recording** | Detector frozen Nov 4 scored the Nov 9 take: 50/50 chewing windows, 0/88 false (§4) |
| Chewing rate can be read during chewing | **One recording** | Earlier measurement: within 2.5% of 45 hand-marked chews (Nov 8) |
| It tells *eating* from other jaw activity (gum, talking, drinking) | **Unsupported** | 12 labelled talking windows; no gum or drinking recordings |
| It works in daily life (walking, commuting, hours of wear) | **Unsupported** | No free-living recording; development has 0.8 hours of not-chewing time, activity undescribed |
| It works for soft food | **Unknown** | Food type is not recorded for any 2025 meal |
| It works for another person or another AirPods fit | **Unknown** | One wearer |
| Bites, swallows, individual chew counts | **Unsupported** | No development recording has hand-marked chews |

Likely limits of the signal, from how it works (§3):
- **Chewing without food looks like eating.** Mimed chewing registered as a
  2.1 Hz rhythm (retired recording, Nov 9). Gum should look the same. Motion
  can only separate these by structure over minutes (bites, pauses, an end),
  not by the chewing itself.
- **Swallowing and drinking** have no rhythm and little jaw travel. Nothing
  in the recordings shows they are visible, and nothing was tested.
- **What was eaten** is not observable. Texture may be, as amplitude.

## 2. Is the score real?

Gradient boosting on the 80 window features, development data: 23
recordings, 6,406 labelled windows, 1,145 chewing.

**The windows are not the sample. Sittings are.** Recordings made back to
back share a meal, an AirPods fit and a posture. The 23 development
recordings are **12 sittings**, and the 8 with chewing are **5 sittings**
(the four `Eating-20251203-06*` files are one meal).

| Check | Result | Reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Folds split one meal across fit and held-out data | AUC-PR 0.948 by recording, 0.945 by sitting, 0.936 leaving one sitting out | Not inflating the score |
| Threshold chosen on the windows it is scored on | F1 0.880 in-sample, **0.855** with the threshold from other folds (0.816 leaving one sitting out) | Reported F1 was about 0.02–0.05 high |
| Intervals resampled recordings as if independent | F1 interval 0.77–0.92 by recording, **0.73–0.93** by sitting | Uncertainty was understated |
| Model keys on the recording's context, not chewing | Pause-in-meal vs no-food recording: AUC-ROC 0.54 | No context shortcut detected by this probe |
| Chewing vs pauses inside held-out meals | AUC-ROC 0.965; null mean 0.49, largest 0.64, p = 0.002 | Genuine, not an artefact of long label runs |
| Never saw a pause inside a meal | False-positive rate on pauses 7.2% (5.9% when trained with them) | Pauses are handled without being taught |
| Trained on meals only | False-positive rate in no-food recordings **12.1%** (2.9% when trained with them) | Unseen activities raise false alarms about fourfold |
| Legacy label clocks (2025, unverified) | Implied lag 0.0 to +1.0 s, median +0.5 s; Nov 9 take 0.0 s | Inside the ±1 s guard, so usable |
| Same sitting in development and test | `Not-eating-20251212-183548` (test) was recorded minutes after six development recordings | That test recording is not a new-sitting test |

Per held-out sitting (threshold from the other sittings):

| Sitting | Recordings | Recall | False-positive rate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meal, Nov 23 2025 | 1 | 0.79 | 4.2% |
| Meal, Nov 26 2025 | 1 | 0.92 | 8.4% |
| Meal, Nov 27 2025 | 1 | 0.83 | 6.7% |
| Meal, Dec 3 2025 | 4 | 0.80 | 4.4% |
| Guided baseline, Nov 9 2026 | 1 | 1.00 | 0.0% |
| Seven no-food sittings | 15 | — | 0.0–1.9%, except Dec 12 evening 7.6% |

One decision per recording (decoded chewing totals at least 10 s): all 8
eating recordings flagged, 1 of 15 no-food recordings flagged
(`Not-eating-20251212-175356`, 22–26 s; no video exists to say what
happened). With 15 recordings, that rate is between 0.2% and 32%.

More meals of the same kind add little: held-out AUC-PR is 0.88 with one
meal sitting in training, 0.94 with two or three, 0.97 with four (spread
0.12 with one, 0.02–0.05 from two up). Variety is what is missing, not volume.

The threshold-from-other-folds estimate removes direct threshold selection
on the scored fold. It is **not fully nested cross-validation**: the other
folds' prediction models can have trained on the scored fold. The F1 interval
above resamples sittings at the in-sample development threshold; it is not an
interval around the threshold-from-other-folds F1.

## 3. What the detector responds to

It is a **vibration detector**, not a rhythm detector.

| Windows given to the model | AUC-PR | Within-meal AUC-ROC |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everything | 0.936 | 0.965 |
| Only content **above 5 Hz** | **0.944** | 0.970 |
| Only content above 12 Hz | 0.879 | 0.969 |
| Only content below 12 Hz | 0.905 | 0.951 |
| Only content below 5 Hz | 0.806 | 0.902 |
| Only content below 2.5 Hz (the chewing rhythm) | 0.665 | 0.880 |
| Only the rhythm features | 0.453 | 0.765 |

Median 12–25 Hz amplitude (three axes combined):

| Context | Accelerometer (mg) | Gyro (mrad/s) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chewing, 2025 meals | 14.9 | 38.5 |
| Pause inside 2025 meals | 3.4 | 7.6 |
| No-food recordings, 2025 | 2.9 | 6.4 |
| Chewing, Nov 9 take | 22.6 | 50.2 |
| Talking, Nov 9 take (12 windows) | 11.3 | 26.4 |
| Nod marker, Nov 9 take | 2.8 | 6.3 |
| Sitting still, Nov 9 take | 2.5 | 4.9 |

Consequences:
- **Talking is the nearest confound**: half of chewing's amplitude in the
  same band, four times above rest. The 12 windows available were all
  classified correctly; that is far too few to rely on.
- **Head movement is not** (nods carry almost nothing above 12 Hz), which
  matches the earlier walking and head-shake results.
- **Soft food is the open risk.** If the vibration comes from teeth and
  food breaking, quiet food should be detected less well. Untested.
- **Detection and pace need different signals.** Detection uses 5–25 Hz;
  the chewing rate is read from 0.5–3 Hz gyro inside a detected bout. The
  existing split between the bout detector and `chew-count-eval` is right.
- **AirPods deliver 50 Hz**, so 25 Hz is the ceiling. The evidence sits at
  the top of the available band.

Simplest models, leaving one sitting out:

| Model | AUC-PR | F1, threshold from other sittings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One number: 12–25 Hz gyro amplitude | AUC-ROC 0.92 | — |
| Logistic regression, 6 band amplitudes | 0.866 | 0.726 |
| Logistic regression, 80 features | 0.916 | 0.758 |
| **Gradient boosting, 80 features** | **0.936** | **0.816** |

Gradient boosting stays: the simpler models lose most in no-food
recordings. The deep model (causal TCN) scored AUC-PR 0.900 against 0.945
in the reference run and has more false bouts (13 vs 9); nothing supports
replacing the feature model with it, or with anything larger, on 5 meal
sittings.

## 4. Forward test of the Live detector

`eating-hgb-20261104-015911-7a28dd15` was frozen on Nov 4 and trained on 2025
recordings only. The Nov 9 baseline was recorded later, with Collect 1.2 on
the shared clock, and is a development recording. Replayed once through the
same streaming code Live runs, at the detector's own threshold (0.71):

| | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Chewing windows found | 50 of 50 |
| False windows | 0 of 88 (rest 37, nods 29, talking 12, stop-chewing 10) |
| Chewing bouts | 2 of 2, no false bout |

One recording. The ledger (`chewsense-motion eating-prospective`) scores
every later development recording once, before it joins training, and never
rescores it. A publication review fixed eligibility to compare absolute
capture times with UTC training completion, excluding ambiguous local times
and legacy migration dates. Existing ledger entries remain unchanged.

## 5. Pipeline audit

### Confirmed problems

1. **The resampling grid changes the evidence band (fixed for training, open
   in Live).**
   - AirPods samples arrive on a steady 20 ms clock (interval spread 0.009 ms).
     Windows are resampled to a 50 Hz grid that ends at the window's nominal
     end time, which falls an arbitrary fraction of a sample away from the
     measurements.
   - Linear interpolation then damps 12–25 Hz content by a factor that depends
     only on that fraction: the median recording varies **1.7×** in amplitude
     (largest 2.0×) between the best and worst offset.
   - The AirPods clock runs 15–45 parts per million slow against the phone
     (14 of the 15 recordings with a long enough unbroken stretch; one reads
     160), so in Live the fraction sweeps through a full cycle about every
     10 minutes (median 10.5, range 8–22).
   - Effect: a model trained on today's windows, scored on windows that sit
     on the samples (where Live starts every session), keeps its ranking
     (AUC-ROC 0.976) but its false-positive rate rises from **3.3% to 7.2%**
     at the same threshold, recall from 0.88 to 0.94.
   - Training on sample-aligned windows scores the same within noise (AUC-PR
     0.941 vs 0.945). So alignment costs nothing and removes the drift.
   - Done: `--window-grid sample` in the experiment. Not done: the Swift
     runtime. Export refuses a sample-grid run until Live implements it.
2. **Development F1 used an in-sample threshold** (§2). Now also reported
   with the threshold chosen on other folds.
3. **Intervals treated recordings of one sitting as independent** (§2).
   Folds and intervals now use sittings by default.
4. **One locked-test negative shares a sitting with development data** (§2).
   The split is immutable, so it is listed in every report instead.
5. **Vision misses most closed-lip chewing.** On the Nov 9 take it proposed
   5 cycles where the chewing rate implies about 50. Agreement proposals
   therefore covered 2 s of 35 s of chewing; the wearer still had to mark
   the rest. All four agreement spans were correct to within 0.7 s at
   their edges.

### Suspected, not confirmed

- **New recordings in one sitting are split between training and evaluation**
  by a per-recording hash. This lets the test contain the same fit and meal
  as training. Measured effect in development is nil (§2), so it was left
  alone; it is a policy choice (below).
- **`Not-eating-20251212-175356` may contain chewing or talking.** Two
  different models flag the same 20–25 s. No video exists.
- **The TCN's earlier failure on 2026 field tests** was not re-examined: those
  recordings are locked test. On the Nov 9 take it found all chewing.

### Verified strengths

- Stored development predictions of `eating-20261108-105237` are reproduced
  by today's code to 1e-16 (6,649 windows).
- Reference run repeated (`eating-20261110-102927` and `-151121`): same
  fingerprint and identical metrics for all five models. The study itself
  was run three times with identical numbers.
- No duplicate motion files across splits (content hashes).
- Label tiers, guard bands and gap masking behave as documented.
- Guided instructions and the wearer's own marks agree within 1.1 s on the
  Nov 9 take (two bouts), so instruction-derived labels are plausible with
  the ±1 s guard. Two bouts are not enough to settle it.
- Units are consistent across 2025 and 2026 recordings (user acceleration in
  g without gravity, rotation rate in rad/s).

## 7. Experiments, most informative first

### A. Confound and texture sitting (needs you, about 15 minutes)

- **Hypothesis.** The detector responds to 5–25 Hz jaw vibration. So: soft
  food is detected less well than crunchy food; talking, gum and drinking
  raise false alarms in proportion to their 12–25 Hz amplitude; head
  movement does not.
- **Data.** One sitting with the existing guided experiments: Soft Food,
  Crunchy Food, Talking, Drinking, Gum Chewing. Then, on another day, one
  complete Natural Meal v2.
- **Procedure.** Record and send as usual. Run
  `chewsense-motion eating-prospective`: the frozen Live detector scores each
  take once, before any retraining.
- **Metrics.** Recall per food; false-positive rate per activity at the
  fixed threshold 0.71; 12–25 Hz amplitude per context.
- **Expected.** Crunchy recall above 0.9; soft lower; talking some false
  alarms; gum detected as chewing.
- **Failure criteria.** Soft-food recall under 0.6, or talking false-positive
  rate over 20%.
- **What it settles.** Whether "chewing detected" can mean "eating" for this
  wearer, and which confound needs a second cue. It says nothing about other
  people.

### B. Align the Live grid (no recording needed)

- **Hypothesis.** With sample-aligned windows in training and in Live, the
  operating point no longer drifts with the clock.
- **Procedure.** Implement the grid in `EatingFeatureExtractor`/runtime behind
  a specification field, re-export from a `--window-grid sample` run, check
  Swift/Python parity, replay a saved Live check.
- **Failure.** Parity not bit-identical, or development AUC-PR drops by more
  than 0.02.
- **Needs** your approval: it replaces the detector bundled in Live.

### C. Second wearer (later)

Nothing about other people can be said until one other person records the
90-Second Baseline twice. Score with `eating-prospective` before training.

### Decision for you: split by sitting

New recordings are assigned to training or evaluation one by one. Takes from
one sitting can land on both sides. Keeping a sitting together makes the test
stricter (new fit, new meal) but leaves fewer test recordings. The measured
effect in development is nil, so this is not urgent.

## 8. Remaining uncertainties

- Everything about other people, other AirPods and other fits.
- Soft food, gum, drinking, talking while eating, free-living wear.
- Whether the 3.3% → 7.2% false-alarm drift occurs in Live as predicted; it
  was computed from recordings, not observed on the phone.
- Whether individual chews can be timed from motion. No development recording
  has hand-marked chews; the 45 that exist are locked test.
- The locked test has 3 recordings with chewing. Its interval (F1 0.51–0.98)
  will stay wide until more evaluation recordings exist.
